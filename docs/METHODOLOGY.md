# Metkagram Mark–Frame Method

Status: canonical methodological description.

## Name

The learning methodology is called the **Metkagram Mark–Frame Method**.

The short explanatory sequence is:

> **see the sentence → notice the Mark → extract the Frame → understand the Move → discriminate → retrieve → bridge → reuse**

The name is intentionally descriptive rather than pseudo-scientific. `Frame`, `Move`, retrieval and transfer are established ideas used in language learning and linguistics. Metkagram's distinctive design is the way these layers are connected around **inline visual Marks inside complete language examples** and stable reusable learning objects.

Do not describe the individual words `Frame` or `Move` as Metkagram inventions. Describe the **Mark–Frame workflow and its implementation** as the project-specific method.

## The method

### 1. See

Start with a complete sentence or short piece of real language. Meaning remains visible before abstraction.

### 2. Mark

Place small reviewed visual cues directly inside the language example. A Mark helps the learner notice a structural or functional role without replacing the sentence with a detached grammar diagram.

Annotation is a support mechanism, not a requirement for every target language.

### 3. Frame

Extract the reusable language-specific structure from the example. The Frame should be reusable across contexts and should preserve the constraints that matter.

A Frame belongs to one learning language.

### 4. Move

Connect the Frame to the communicative or reasoning job it performs: correcting a framing, hedging, setting a condition, challenging an assumption, drawing a conclusion and so on.

A Move is language-independent. Different languages may realise the same Move through different Frames.

### 5. Discriminate

Use reviewed Contrasts to show where nearby Frames differ. The goal is not to produce a giant similarity network. The learner needs distinctions that change an actual language decision.

### 6. Retrieve

Use Choices before explanation. The learner commits to a Frame for a bounded situation, then sees feedback and the reviewed Contrast behind it.

### 7. Route

When one communicative job requires several related objects, order them into a short Route. A Route references canonical objects rather than cloning their content.

### 8. Bridge

Connect reviewed Frames across learning languages when they perform the same or a closely related Move. A Bridge records functional correspondence, not automatic translation equivalence.

### 9. Reuse

Return to production: adapt the Frame to a new sentence, situation or language task.

## Two loops, not one giant funnel

The system has two useful loops.

### Core noticing loop

**Sentence → Mark → Frame → Move → reuse**

This is the smallest expression of the original Metkagram idea and should remain understandable even if the learner never opens the more advanced tools.

### Retrieval and transfer loop

**Frame → Contrast → Choice → Route / Bridge → reuse**

This layer turns the curriculum into a decision and transfer system instead of a passive catalogue.

## Methodological boundaries

Metkagram should not claim that visual Marks, Frames or retrieval automatically improve learning outcomes merely because the architecture is plausible. Product design and research evidence are separate things.

Public wording should therefore distinguish:

- **method description** — what Metkagram does;
- **design rationale** — why the mechanism is plausible or useful;
- **research hypothesis** — what should be tested;
- **evidence** — what a study actually demonstrated.

This keeps the project credible as its Research section grows.

## Multilingual consequence

The Mark–Frame Method is deliberately not annotation-first at the architecture level.

A new learning language can begin with:

1. reviewed Frames and examples;
2. one or more translation locales;
3. Moves and optional Contrasts;
4. Bridges to other learning languages;
5. annotation Marks later, when a reliable profile exists.

That preserves the original visual method where it is available without making an NLP pipeline a gatekeeper for every new language.

## Public one-liners

Long:

> **Metkagram starts with complete language, places small Marks inside the sentence, turns recurring structure into reusable Frames, connects them to communicative Moves, and trains retrieval and cross-language reuse through Contrasts, Choices, Routes and Bridges.**

Short:

> **See the structure. Keep the Frame. Reuse the Move.**
