# Thinking in Language

Status: two additive curriculum layers implemented.

Metkagram can teach more than isolated grammar forms. A reusable Frame can also preserve a useful reasoning operation: frame a problem, separate evidence from assumptions, trace system effects, diagnose causes, test explanations, represent another viewpoint fairly, or learn from feedback.

## Layer 1 · Frame and decide

- **FRM — Problem framing and reframing**: define the problem, expose the constraint, and choose a more useful frame before solving.
- **UNC — Reasoning under uncertainty**: separate evidence from assumptions, calibrate confidence, and ask for discriminating evidence.
- **SYS — Systems thinking**: trace second-order effects, bottlenecks, local optimisation, symptoms, and structural drivers.
- **DEC — Decision quality and trade-offs**: set criteria first, use reversibility, compare the cost of delay, distinguish risk profiles, and preserve optionality.

## Layer 2 · Diagnose, test, understand, learn

- **CDG — Causal diagnosis**: separate a trigger from an underlying cause, compare competing mechanisms, make intermediate mechanisms visible, and distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions.
- **HYP — Hypothesis testing**: turn explanations into predictions, state what evidence would change the conclusion, compare alternatives, and choose high-information tests.
- **PST — Perspective taking**: state the strongest reasonable objection, separate positions from interests, distinguish facts from values, and reformulate disagreement around the actual trade-off.
- **META — Metacognitive learning**: distinguish recognition from recall, identify a precise confusion, find recurring error patterns, and turn feedback into the next bounded practice target.

Each set starts with five curated English/German Frames, Russian learner-support translations, examples, and reasoning metadata. The content is deliberately additive: established study sets remain part of the public curriculum.

## Editorial rule

A thinking Frame belongs here only when it does two jobs at once:

1. it is a natural, reusable language pattern worth learning at B2–C1;
2. it encodes a reasoning operation that a learner can reuse in another situation.

The goal is not to turn Metkagram into a glossary of clever terminology. The learner should be able to use the pattern in a meeting, an essay, a technical discussion, research, or an ordinary decision and gain a clearer way to structure the thought.

## Product rule

Curriculum growth and evaluation evidence are separate concerns. New Frames may expand the public curriculum, Pattern Atlas and machine-readable domain model, but they do not silently rewrite frozen benchmark totals or historical evidence claims. A new evaluation claim needs its own reviewed benchmark update.

## Next expansion candidates

The next reviewed layer should go deeper rather than merely wider. Strong candidates are strategic sequencing, counterfactual diagnosis, forecasting and calibration, argument repair, prioritisation under constraints, and transfer from feedback into deliberate practice. New sets should extend the curriculum rather than consolidate it by deletion.
