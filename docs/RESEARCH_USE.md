# Research use and collaboration

Metkagram is an applied language-learning and NLP research project. Its central research object is a concrete system of readable sentence-level functional annotation connected to reusable language patterns, variation, retrieval practice, and machine-readable provenance.

The project is public so its claims, examples, data structures, and research direction can be inspected. Public visibility does not make the corpus or implementation unrestricted research data.

## Research use that does not need a separate agreement

You may, subject to applicable law and the repository [LICENSE](../LICENSE):

- read and evaluate the public website;
- link to public Metkagram pages;
- cite the project and individual canonical records;
- discuss the method, hypotheses, strengths, and limitations;
- use limited quotations where applicable law permits them.

## Research use that needs permission first

Please request written permission before a project relies on substantial Metkagram material for:

- downloading or retaining a substantial corpus copy;
- distributing stimuli or source records to participants or collaborators;
- publishing substantial source material as a supplement;
- producing or releasing a derived annotation dataset;
- training, fine-tuning, or adapting an ML/NLP model on Metkagram material;
- constructing a benchmark, retrieval corpus, or evaluation set from substantial Metkagram material;
- reproducing the Metkagram implementation or annotation interface;
- combining the corpus into another dataset that will be redistributed.

A research permission may allow these activities for a named project without granting general reuse rights.

## What a proposal should include

Send a short proposal with:

1. researcher(s), institution, lab, or company;
2. project title and research question;
3. study design or technical use;
4. exact Metkagram data/components requested;
5. expected number of records or participants;
6. whether data will leave the research team;
7. whether ML/LLM training, fine-tuning, embeddings, RAG, or evaluation is involved;
8. intended publication, thesis, demo, or product output;
9. funding and commercial context;
10. proposed citation and attribution.

## Collaboration models

Possible arrangements include:

- **Reproducible pilot.** A preregistered learning experiment with a defined subset of Metkagram stimuli.
- **Annotation study.** Inter-annotator agreement, schema review, error analysis, or comparison with another annotation framework.
- **NLP evaluation.** A controlled experiment using a licensed subset for parsing, classification, retrieval, or representation research.
- **Student project.** A supervised thesis or course study with a bounded dataset and clear attribution.
- **Joint publication.** Co-designed research where methodology, data provenance, and contribution roles are agreed before the work starts.
- **Commercial R&D evaluation.** Time- or dataset-bounded evaluation before a separate production/data license.

## Evidence boundary

Metkagram distinguishes three things that are easy to blur together:

1. findings established in prior cognitive or second-language research;
2. Metkagram's design interpretation of those findings;
3. effects that have actually been measured for the Metkagram implementation.

The project should not claim category 3 on the basis of categories 1 or 2. Research collaborations should preserve this distinction and report null or negative results as seriously as positive results.

## Research identity and provenance

The repository history, versioned datasets, stable record identifiers, content hashes, and canonical URLs are part of the project's provenance trail. When publishing work based on Metkagram, record the dataset version and the exact subset used whenever possible.

Recommended project citation:

> Metkagram (2026). *Metkagram: functional language annotation and reusable pattern corpus.* https://metkagram.github.io/

For a particular pattern or annotated record, also cite its canonical URL.

## No exclusivity by accident

A research permission is project-specific unless a written agreement says otherwise. Participation in a study does not automatically transfer ownership of Metkagram's pre-existing code, corpus, notation, schemas, or documentation to a collaborator, institution, funder, or publisher.

Likewise, Metkagram should not claim ownership of a collaborator's pre-existing methods, code, or data. Jointly created outputs should have ownership and publication terms agreed before substantial work begins.
