# Metkagram method provenance

## Purpose

This document records what Metkagram is actually developing and what it does **not** claim to have invented. It exists for research provenance, citation, collaboration, and IP clarity.

## The Metkagram contribution

Metkagram develops a concrete applied system in which:

1. a natural sentence remains readable as a whole;
2. compact functional marks are attached directly to the relevant word or span;
3. the marked sentence can be represented as structured token/span data rather than only rendered HTML;
4. recurring structures are extracted into reusable patterns;
5. patterns are varied across languages, persons, tenses, polarity, questions, and contexts;
6. examples, translations, functional roles, formulas, identifiers, quality metadata, and provenance can be connected in one corpus;
7. the same structured material can support language learning, NLP analysis, reproducible experiments, search, and agent/API access.

The distinctive object is therefore not a single tag such as `S` or `V`. It is the **specific notation, data model, corpus, mappings, selection and arrangement, visual presentation, pattern system, research protocol, and implementation that connect sentence-level cues to reusable structures**.

## What Metkagram does not claim

Metkagram does not claim to have invented:

- grammatical annotation;
- parts of speech or syntactic roles;
- color coding or visual cues in education;
- chunking or construction-based language learning;
- retrieval practice or spaced repetition;
- corpus linguistics;
- token/span annotation in NLP;
- multilingual examples or translations;
- the general idea of extracting patterns from sentences.

Those concepts have independent histories. Metkagram's research contribution is the concrete way they are combined, represented, curated, and tested in this project.

## Protectable project assets

Depending on applicable law and originality, project assets may include copyrightable or otherwise protected expression such as:

- source code and build tooling;
- annotation definitions, documentation, and examples;
- the concrete annotation schema and machine-readable representation;
- selection, arrangement, and curation of corpus material;
- reusable pattern collections and authored explanations;
- translations and editorial material where rights are held by Metkagram;
- visual notation, layouts, diagrams, and design assets;
- research protocols, reports, and documentation;
- API schemas, provenance metadata, and generated project-specific datasets.

Third-party material remains subject to third-party rights.

## Ideas versus implementation

The public description of a method does not turn the underlying abstract idea into a copyright monopoly. Metkagram therefore relies on a combination of:

- clear authorship and repository history;
- copyright in concrete works;
- explicit licensing boundaries;
- provenance and stable versioning;
- controlled permission for substantial research/data reuse;
- keeping genuinely confidential future know-how outside the public repository when secrecy matters;
- separate agreements for research and commercial partnerships.

This is more defensible than claiming ownership of broad linguistic concepts.

## Versioned evidence

Git history, release dates, stable identifiers, dataset fingerprints, canonical URLs, and archived research notes form a provenance trail showing how the system evolved. Material changes to the annotation scheme or research method should be committed with descriptive messages rather than silently overwritten.

For any formal priority, patentability, trademark, database-right, or jurisdiction-specific question, qualified IP counsel should review the facts before a public disclosure or transaction.
